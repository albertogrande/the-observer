# You Can't Prove Claude Got Worse. That's the Problem.

*Week of 2026-08-17 to 2026-08-23 · Three tools to make Claude shut up shipped in three days. When the models are a tie, trust is the product — and this week it leaked.*

Three days, three tools, all built to do the same thing: make Claude stop talking.

On the 20th, [Vomit](https://github.com/zachahn/vomit) hit Hacker News (303 points) — a wrapper that pipes Claude 5's output through a *second* model to strip the preamble and narration. The next day, [nobuzz](https://github.com/adnanakil/nobuzz) landed (352 points): "Make Claude stop talking like a BuzzFeed article." The day after that, a satirical [English ↔ Claudish translator](https://programasweights.com/claudish). Same week, a widely-read [switching audit](https://allaboutcoding.ghinda.com/a-week-of-using-codex-more-than-claude/) (230 points) explained why its author moved daily work to OpenAI's Codex — routine multi-step tasks finished in about 20 minutes there versus roughly two hours on Opus 5, and Claude had, in his words, an "infuriating passive aggressive sulky know it all personality." And an [essay on "AI-blindness"](https://cymerys.com/w/im-becoming-ai-blind) (487 points) drew this from its author in the comments: "Claude has become noticeably, painfully worse at writing in the last six months. At this point it's practically useless for anything except code."

Anthropic ships the best coding model most weeks. Its users spent this week building software to route around it. That gap is the story.

## When the models tie, trust is the product

Start with what we established [last week](./2026-W33.md). Six frontier-grade coding models shipped in seven days, most open-weight, all clustered within a few points of each other — and the benchmarks that would rank them [have stopped resolving them](./deep-dives/2026-08-05-benchmark-saturation-margin-of-error.md). When the top SWE-bench scores sit inside the benchmark's own error bar, the model is a commodity. The buyer stops choosing on capability, because capability is a tie, and starts choosing on everything else: price, latency, and the part no leaderboard measures — how it *feels* to work with.

That last axis is where the whole fight goes when the others flatten. We called it [competing on the un-measured margin](./2026-W33.md). This week it turned on Anthropic.

Here is the sharpest version of the problem, and it isn't the verbosity. Late in the week a developer [posted](https://x.com/argofowl/status/2091150597374537729) that Anthropic appeared to be A/B testing *reduced effort levels* in Claude Code — quietly serving some sessions a dialed-down model without a changelog line. It reached the HN front page (196 points). Read the evidence and it's thin: it rests on one user, and his method was to ask Claude what effort level it was running at — which, as the top skeptic [put it](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49401549), assumes "the model even know[s] that." Anthropic's Claude Code team [replied directly](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49401549): yes, an experiment is running that "maps the numerical effort value differently," but "the effort you selected is the effort you're getting." An A/B test on the *labels*, they say — not the delivered work.

So: unproven. Probably even untrue in the strong form. And it hit the front page anyway, because a few hundred developers read "Anthropic is secretly degrading the model you're paying for" and found it entirely plausible. That plausibility is the asset that leaked. You cannot prove Claude got worse this week. You also cannot prove it didn't — the config is server-side, the effort scale is opaque, and the only instrument you have is your own gut. When a vendor's users can't tell whether the product was nerfed, and their prior is "probably," the trust is already gone. The ambiguity *is* the deficit.

## The verbosity is the part you can measure

The one thing that isn't ambiguous is the tone, and Anthropic confirmed it — by shipping a fix. Claude Code [v2.1.237](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/releases/tag/v2.1.237), shipped midweek, added a built-in **"Concise" output style** that "leads with results and skips preamble." You don't ship an anti-preamble mode unless the preamble is a known problem. The three community tools are the same admission from the other side: [one commenter](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49388752) called it "a sad indictment of Anthropic's product that so many people hate interacting with it." Another, on Vomit's thread, was blunter — the style is "so obnoxious it's impacting my work."

This is not a cosmetic complaint. It compounds directly with the [dive we ran on Opus 5](./deep-dives/2026-08-15-model-didnt-get-worse-stopped-asking.md): earlier Claudes would stop and ask when your intent was unclear; Opus 5 guesses boldly instead, because a single-shot benchmark scores a clarifying question at zero. Train hard on the leaderboard and you optimize away exactly the behaviors — asking, brevity, restraint — that make a tool feel trustworthy in daily use and that no public eval rewards. The verbosity backlash and the "it stopped asking" complaint are the same regression, viewed from two chairs. Anthropic won the number and shipped the feel that makes people build wrappers.

## The watermark is working for someone who isn't you

The third thread this week sharpens the trust point to its edge. Anthropic [confirmed](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark) it now watermarks Claude's *text* output — a semantic scheme that biases word choice toward a secret "green list" so the output is statistically detectable, with no visible characters. John Gruber's reaction, the week before, [named the stake](https://daringfireball.net/2026/08/anthropics_watermark_text_adulteration_in_claude_is_a_perversion_of_writing): "The idea that anything other than my needs should factor into the generation of text for me is patently offensive." The model is now shading its words for the benefit of a downstream detector, not the person who asked.

The honest complication landed this week too. A [detection quiz](https://sgoedecke.github.io/watermark-quiz/) went up this week; players scored around 3 out of 10 — at or below chance. The watermark is real and statistically measurable, but humanly imperceptible; "utterly imperceptible," one player [wrote](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49374729), "even when studied under the microscope." So the quality harm Gruber fears is smaller than the rhetoric. But the *governance* signal is exactly his: the vendor shaded the words for a purpose other than your own, silently, inside the text you're paying it to write for you. Add that to an effort A/B test with no changelog and a preamble everyone hates, and the pattern isn't "Claude got worse." It's "Claude is being adjusted, quietly, in ways I can't see or verify." That is a trust problem wearing three costumes.

## Why this is Anthropic's problem specifically

Every lab does silent A/B tests. Every model is verbose. So why did this crest on Anthropic this week? Because Anthropic's entire market position is the *premium* one. It charges more and sells the promise that the extra dollars buy a model you can hand real work to and trust. In [W29](./2026-W29.md) we watched Microsoft coach its salesforce to sell *against* that promise — "model quality is beside the point." The commodity floor keeps rising to meet it. When your one differentiator is "trust us, ours is better to work with," a week of your own users publishing tools to make it shut up, plus a switching diary to the cheaper competitor, plus an unfalsifiable degradation rumor you can only answer with "trust us," is not a PR problem. It's the differentiator itself corroding.

The through-line from [W32's mood](./2026-W32.md) to here: the benchmark can't referee capability anymore, so the market runs on feel — and feel runs on trust, which is the one asset you can't benchmark, can't patch in a point release, and spend faster than you notice.

## What the reader should do

You use Claude Code every day. Three concrete moves.

Turn on the **Concise output style** now (`/config` → Output style). It shipped this week for exactly this, and it's the one part of the complaint Anthropic already fixed.

Don't trust the vibes, and don't dismiss them either — instrument them. The argofowl episode is the lesson: neither the "they nerfed it" crowd nor Anthropic's "the effort you selected is what you're getting" can be checked from the outside. The only answer is your own [small fixture set](./deep-dives/2026-07-20-cheapest-adequate-model.md) — a handful of your real tasks you re-run across versions and vendors, measuring [cost-per-solved-task](./2026-W33.md), not cost-per-token or gut feel. It's the only instrument that tells you whether a model moved. Build it before the next version bump, because you'll want it the day you suspect a regression and have nothing but a hunch.

And keep the Codex session warm. Not because it's better — it's a tie, that's the point — but because the week your vendor is spending trust is the week to have a measured, working fallback. Portability was thrift in June. Now it's the receipt you keep on the vendor's behavior.

## One thing to watch

The verbosity backlash is cheap to answer and expensive to ignore, so I expect Anthropic to answer it at the product level — not leave de-verbosing to opt-in modes and third-party wrappers.

**Prediction (60% confident):** By 2026-11-23, Anthropic makes a low-preamble output the *default* in at least one first-party surface — Claude Code's default output style flips to a Concise-style, or the API/system prompt trims narration by default — rather than keeping it an opt-in toggle. If instead the default stays verbose and the fix stays opt-in through then, I'm wrong that the trust cost was large enough to force a default change.
